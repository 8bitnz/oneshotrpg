# PROJECT: EMBERVALE — A Retro Party RPG

## Mission
Build a complete, polished, fully playable 2D top-down turn-based party RPG that runs in the browser (HTML/JS/Canvas, no build tools, no external assets — everything procedurally generated or hand-coded). The spiritual heir of early Ultima and Shadowlands: a big open world you get *lost* in, a hero and companions, towns with life in them, dungeons with secrets, and a separate tactical combat screen.

This is a long-run project. Work autonomously through the milestones below. Do not stop at "it runs" — stop when it meets the Definition of Done. Gameplay is king. Graphics are crisp 8-bit pixel art, but depth beats decoration every time.

## Design Pillars (consult these when making any tradeoff)
1. **Getting lost is the point.** A big open world with landmarks, rumors, and places the player discovers rather than is directed to. Light main plot, heavy sense of place.
2. **The party is the story.** A hero plus recruitable companions with names, personalities, banter, and mechanical identities. Losing one should hurt.
3. **Combat is a puzzle, not a slog.** Hybrid combat: tactical grid positioning + action menus. Every fight screen is its own little chess problem.
4. **Respect the player's imagination.** Terse, evocative text like old CRPGs. Rumors in taverns. Cryptic signposts. No hand-holding, no quest markers.
5. **Juice within the 8-bit budget.** Screen shake, hit flashes, chiptune stings, damage numbers — polish that makes actions feel good without breaking the retro aesthetic.

## Technical Constraints
- Single-page app: `index.html` + JS modules + one CSS file. Runs by opening index.html or via a trivial static server. No frameworks, no bundlers, no CDN dependencies.
- Canvas rendering at a fixed internal resolution (e.g., 320×180 or 384×216), integer-scaled to fit the window. Crisp pixels: `image-rendering: pixelated`, no anti-aliasing.
- All sprites generated in code (draw pixel-art to offscreen canvases at load, or embed tiny sprite definitions as data). Consistent 16×16 tile / sprite grid, a coherent limited palette (~16-24 colors, DB16 or similar).
- All music and SFX via Web Audio API chiptunes (square/triangle/noise channels, sequenced loops). Distinct themes: overworld, town, dungeon, combat, victory, game over. SFX for menus, hits, spells, footsteps, level-up, gold.
- Save/load via localStorage with at least 3 slots + autosave. NOTE: develop and test saves defensively — save data must survive version changes gracefully (version the schema).
- Keyboard-first controls (arrows/WASD + Z/X/Enter/Esc style), with mouse support in menus and combat. Show controls in-game.
- 60fps target; keep the world chunked/culled so the big map stays fast.

## Core Systems

### World & Exploration
- Overworld map: large (at least 200×200 tiles), handcrafted layout generated by code at build time with a fixed seed so it's the *same* world every playthrough — continents/regions, forests, mountains, swamps, deserts, roads, rivers with bridges, coastlines. Terrain affects encounters and movement.
- 5+ towns/villages and 1 city, each with a distinct identity, shops (weapons, armor, items, magic, inn, temple), and 8-15 NPCs with dialogue. NPCs give rumors that point to real places and secrets.
- 6+ dungeons (caves, crypts, ruined keeps, a final dungeon), each multi-floor with keys/levers/secret doors, traps, treasure, a boss, and one memorable gimmick each. Fog-of-war or darkness + light sources in dungeons.
- Day/night cycle affecting encounters and NPC dialogue. Random encounters on overworld/dungeons (visible or step-based — pick what plays better) plus fixed set-piece fights.
- Fast travel earned late (a ship, moongates, or similar Ultima-flavored system).

### Party & Progression
- Hero created at start (name + class: e.g., Fighter, Rogue, Mage, Cleric-style choices).
- 6+ recruitable companions found in the world (tavern hires, quest rescues, secret characters). Party of up to 4 active. Companions have distinct classes, stats, portraits, join conditions, and occasional interjections in dialogue.
- Classic stat block (STR/DEX/INT/etc.), levels 1-20, XP from combat and quests. Learnable spells/abilities per class — at least 20 spells across schools + 15 martial/rogue abilities.
- Equipment slots (weapon, armor, shield, accessory) with 60+ items including uniques with lore text. Consumables, quest items, gold economy that stays meaningful all game.

### Combat (separate screen, hybrid grid + menus)
- Entering combat transitions to a dedicated battle screen: a tactical grid (~10×8) with terrain features pulled from where you fought (trees, rocks, water).
- Turn order by initiative/speed, visible queue. On a unit's turn: move on the grid (range shown), then choose from an action menu — Attack, Ability/Spell, Item, Defend, Flee. Facing/flanking or back-attack bonuses; AoE spell templates; ranged line-of-sight.
- Enemy variety: 25+ enemy types across regions with real AI differences (archers keep distance, brutes charge, shamans buff/heal, wolves flank). 7+ bosses with phases or gimmicks.
- Death = downed in combat, revivable at temples or via spells; full party wipe = game over (load save).

### Story & Quests (light plot, heavy world)
- A simple, mythic main thread (an old evil stirring, 5-7 major beats) that mostly exists to give the exploration direction and a finale.
- 15+ side quests discovered through play — overheard rumors, notes on corpses, strange map features — not a quest log full of exclamation points. A minimal journal that records clues verbatim.
- Environmental storytelling: ruins that imply history, gravestones, books on shelves, NPCs who contradict each other.

## Architecture Guidance
- Clean separation: engine (loop, input, renderer, audio, scenes) / systems (world, party, combat, dialogue, inventory, save) / content (data files: maps, NPCs, items, enemies, quests as plain JS data).
- Content as data is critical — it lets you add breadth cheaply in later milestones.
- A scene/state stack (overworld → town → dialogue → shop; anything → combat → results).
- Keep files focused and under ~500 lines where reasonable; refactor when they grow.

## Autonomous Workflow — Milestones
Work in this order. After each milestone: run it, playtest it via the checklist, fix everything broken, commit with a clear message, and write a short STATUS.md entry (what's done, what's next, known issues).

1. **Skeleton** — game loop, scene stack, input, pixel renderer, palette, tile engine, a walkable test map.
2. **Overworld** — full world generation with fixed seed, camera, collision, terrain, day/night, minimap or in-world cartography.
3. **Combat vertical slice** — full hybrid battle screen with 4 party members vs mixed enemies, all action types, one boss. Tune until fights are genuinely fun before moving on.
4. **Party & RPG systems** — stats, XP, classes, spells/abilities, inventory, equipment, character screens.
5. **Towns & dialogue** — town maps, NPCs, dialogue system, shops, inns, temples, rumor system.
6. **Dungeons** — dungeon tileset, darkness/light, traps, keys, secrets, 2 complete dungeons with bosses.
7. **Content pass** — remaining towns, dungeons, companions, side quests, enemies, items, main story beats, finale.
8. **Audio** — chiptune engine, all themes and SFX, volume options.
9. **Polish & juice** — title screen with theme music, transitions, combat feedback effects, balancing pass, save slots, options menu, controls screen, credits.
10. **Hardening** — full playthrough simulation of the critical path, fix soft-locks, edge cases (fleeing, dead party members in menus, full inventory, saving mid-anything).

## Self-Verification Loop (do this every milestone)
- Actually launch and play it (or drive it with a headless/scripted harness where possible). Never assume code works.
- Checklist each milestone: Can I start a new game? Can I reach every implemented area? Can combat be won AND lost cleanly? Does save→reload→continue work from every scene? Any console errors? Is the frame rate solid?
- Write small automated sanity tests for pure logic (damage math, XP curves, pathfinding, save serialization).
- After the content pass, do a balance audit: level curve vs. enemy difficulty by region, gold income vs. shop prices, spell usefulness spread.

## Definition of Done
- A new player can go from title screen to final boss victory (~4-8 hours) with no crashes, soft-locks, or dead ends.
- All systems above exist and interact; nothing is stubbed.
- The world rewards wandering: at least 10 things a player can find that no quest told them about.
- Combat remains interesting at hour 4 (new enemies/abilities still appearing).
- Music and SFX everywhere; the game feels alive.
- Code is organized, data-driven, and STATUS.md documents the final state.

## Anti-Goals
- No modern UI chrome, no tutorials longer than one screen, no quest arrows, no minimaps that trivialize exploration.
- No placeholder art/audio in the final build. No TODOs on the critical path.
- Don't burn time on shaders, particles beyond simple pixel effects, or engine generality the game doesn't need.

Begin with Milestone 1. Keep a running STATUS.md. If you finish everything, spend remaining effort on: more companion banter, more secrets, and combat balance.
